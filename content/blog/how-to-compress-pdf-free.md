## Why compress a PDF?

Large PDFs are hard to email (most providers cap attachments at 10–25 MB), slow to upload to forms and portals, and eat storage on shared drives. Compression reduces file size by re-encoding embedded images at a smaller resolution or lower quality.

## What can actually be compressed?

- **Image-heavy PDFs** (scans, photos, presentations): Large gains, often 50–80% reduction.
- **Text-only PDFs** (contracts, reports): Small gains, the file is already compact.

## Tips for better compression

- **Scanned PDFs**: These compress the most because pages are large JPEGs or PNGs. Use Heavy compression for archive copies.
- **Already compressed**: If someone ran the PDF through iLovePDF or Smallpdf already, there is not much left to squeeze out.
- **After compressing**: If the result is still too large, try [splitting](/split-pdf) the PDF and compressing each half.

## What about text quality?

Text and vector graphics (shapes, charts drawn in software) are **never re-encoded**. They stay crisp at any zoom level. Only raster images embedded in the PDF are affected.
